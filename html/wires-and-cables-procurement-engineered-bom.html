The Challenge

Buying metal and compound off spreadsheet BOMs

With 33,000+ SKUs across house wires, power, control and specialty cables, the BOM was the procurement plan. It was built by hand, one SKU at a time.

A spreadsheet per SKU

Engineers recalculated kg/km by hand for every new SKU or tender. A single BOM took one to two days.

Buffers on buffers

Upper-tolerance thickness plus a flat safety margin at each step. Copper and PVC were over-bought and sat as inventory.

One cable, many BOMs

The same cable code carried different BOMs across plants and files. No single version matched the approved construction.

MRP on stale numbers

Material plans used BOMs that missed spec changes and real scrap rates. The result was compound shortages and spot buys.

HMC Approach

From hand calculation to a governed BOM engine

Engineering owns the physics. Procurement owns the buffers. The engine makes both visible.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Compare the BOM to the shop floor

We sampled 200 high-volume SKUs and checked each BOM against actual consumption.

  • Reconciled BOM kg/km with production issue slips and drum weights.
  • Traced where the margin came from: upper-tolerance thickness, rounded diameters, flat buffers.
  • Mapped the BOM journey from engineering to the plant and on to MRP and the purchase order.

Deliverable: Variance baseline by product family and material.

Design · One calculation logic, one parameter master

Standard construction logic for each family, aligned to the relevant IS specifications.

  • Parameter master: material densities, lay-up factors, nominal thicknesses and armour coverage.
  • Separate policy table for process allowances by plant and material, owned by procurement.
  • BOM versioning rules and an engineering–procurement sign-off workflow.

Deliverable: Calculation rulebook and parameter master.

Build · Engine plus familiar front-end

Python calculates the BOM. Engineers keep working in Excel.

  • Python BOM engine computing kg/km and ₹/km for every layer of the construction.
  • Excel/VBA front-end: pick the construction, get the BOM and an ERP-ready upload sheet.
  • Claude extracts construction parameters from customer tender specs and datasheets into the template.
  • Power Query pulls consumption data; a Power BI dashboard tracks BOM vs actual by SKU.

Deliverable: BOM engine, Excel front-end, variance dashboard.

Adopt · Pilot on LV power cables

The highest-value family went first, with engineering and procurement reviewing together.

  • Parallel run: manual and engineered BOMs side by side for six weeks.
  • A weekly variance review closed gaps in the parameter master.
  • MRP switched to engineered BOMs once variance held below 1.5%.

Deliverable: Pilot sign-off and variance thresholds.

Scale · Every family, every plant

Rolled out family by family, with calibration built into the monthly cycle.

  • House wires, control, flexible, instrumentation and solar cables onboarded.
  • Monthly recalibration of process allowances from actual consumption.
  • A named owner for the parameter master; changes are logged and approved.

Deliverable: Runbook, trained owners, governance cadence.

Key Takeaways

What transfers to any engineered-product buyer

01

Separate physics from policy

Geometry and density are fixed. Buffers are a business decision. Keep them in separate tables so each can be challenged.

02

Calibrate against consumption

Use actual issue data to set allowances. Opinions about scrap are usually higher than scrap.

03

Give the master data an owner

One accountable owner for the parameter master kept 33,000 SKUs consistent long after go-live.
