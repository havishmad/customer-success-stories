The Challenge

Peak season meant queues at the gate and idle docks inside

In AC season (January to April) inbound steel, copper tubes, compressors and electronics roughly double, while the plant ships thousands of AC units a day to brand customers. All of it moves through one inward gate, six docks and one finished-goods yard.

Queues next to empty docks

Supplier trucks waited outside for hours while two of six docks sat idle. Security could see the queue, stores could see the docks, nobody saw both.

Four registers, no single truth

Gate pass, weighbridge slip, GRN and dispatch register each lived in a different book or WhatsApp group. Finding one truck took a round of phone calls.

An overflowing FG yard

Loaded trucks for brand customers waited for documents, seal checks and transporters. The yard filled up and detention charges climbed every peak season.

Lost at shift change

Three shifts, verbal handovers. A dock blocked for GRN at 13:50 was still blocked at 15:00, and the next shift found out when a truck could not dock.

HMC Approach

From gate registers to a twin the whole plant watches

Built with the plant's security, stores, PPC and dispatch teams, starting from their own gate registers, weighbridge slips and GRN timestamps.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Walk the truck

We followed trucks from the highway to the dock and from the line to Gate 2.

  • Time-stamped about 600 truck visits from gate registers, weighbridge slips and GRNs.
  • Split every visit into steps and found where the waiting sat: parking, GRN holds and documents.
  • Mapped the site: gates, weighbridges, 12 parking bays, 6 docks, 24 dispatch bays.

Deliverable: Truck-visit anatomy and a site layout model.

Design · Model the plant

Zones, docks, bays and the rules that move trucks between them.

  • Dock allocation rule: next free dock, yard trucks called in arrival order.
  • Holds and pauses: GRN blocks, forklift holds, shift changeovers.
  • KPI tree agreed with the plant head: turnaround, dock use, yard occupancy, detention.

Deliverable: Twin specification and KPI tree.

Build · A twin you can watch

A 3D model of the whole plant with an event engine underneath.

  • Three.js twin: gates, weighbridges, docks, shops, final line, QA and FG yard.
  • Simulated trucks follow real road paths, queue, reverse into docks and wait for GRN.
  • Built with Claude as the coding partner; dock what-if simulator calibrated on the visit data.

Deliverable: Plant logistics twin (single HTML) and what-if simulator.

Adopt · Run the day from the twin

The twin went on screens at security, stores and the dispatch office.

  • 08:00 dock plan reviewed on the twin each morning.
  • Blocked-dock alerts to stores; yard trucks called by PA and WhatsApp.
  • Auto-drafted shift brief at every handover.

Deliverable: Control-room routine and shift-brief template.

Scale · Live feeds and more plants

From simulated flows to live data, plant by plant.

  • Gate-pass, RFID and weighbridge feeds replace manual entries.
  • Supplier dock-slot booking piloted with copper and compressor vendors.
  • Same twin template for other Amber plants.

Deliverable: Data-feed roadmap and multi-plant template.

Key Takeaways

What transfers to any plant gate

01

Make the waiting visible

Most of a truck's visit was invisible waiting, not slow unloading. Putting every truck on one screen cut it before any new dock was built.

02

Simulate the season, then spend

Running peak arrivals through the simulator showed when a seventh dock pays back and when slot booking alone is enough.

03

Let the twin write the handover

An auto-drafted shift brief kept context across three shifts and gave the plant head one page every morning.
