The Challenge

Thirty-four org charts, and none of them current

Sterlite Power runs transmission projects, conductor and cable plants and an upgrade business across India. People move between projects every quarter. HR's picture of who reports to whom lived in slide decks that went stale the week they were sent.

Charts as slides

Every BU kept its own PowerPoint org chart. Thirty-four versions were in circulation, each drawn by hand and out of date within weeks.

Layers nobody chose

Some plant teams sat eight layers below the CEO, and a quarter of managers had one or two reports. Nobody could see it, so nobody fixed it.

Reorgs on a whiteboard

Each restructuring took weeks of redrawing. Leaders saw one option, late, with no view of the cost, the layers or the people affected.

Succession in a drawer

Successor lists sat in a separate file. When a project director resigned, HR found out on the same day that there was no ready-now cover.

HMC Approach

From 34 slide decks to one living organisation

Built with Sterlite Power's HR leadership and HR business partners, starting from an HRMS extract and the decks they already had.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Count the charts

We gathered every org chart in circulation and compared them with the HRMS.

  • Found 34 versions across BUs, with conflicting reporting lines in a fifth of them.
  • Mapped which HR decisions need an org view: reorgs, spans, succession, hiring.
  • Agreed what must stay private: pay, ratings and risk flags by role.

Deliverable: Org data audit and use-case list.

Design · One Excel, one truth

A single sheet that any HRBP can maintain: one row per person.

  • Columns: ID, name, role, business, reports-to, location, since, grade.
  • Span-health rules agreed: under 3 is narrow, 3 to 10 is healthy, over 10 is wide.
  • Critical-role list and successor readiness added as optional columns.

Deliverable: Org data template and design rules.

Build · The living org

A single HTML file that turns the Excel into an interactive organisation.

  • Org chart with search, filters and detail cards; Excel in and out.
  • Spans-and-layers X-ray, reorg sandbox and succession bench view.
  • Built with Claude as the coding partner; plain-English "Ask the org" questions.

Deliverable: Org Studio (single HTML) and data template.

Adopt · HRBPs own the sheet

The chart refreshes the day the Excel changes.

  • HRBPs update their BU tab; the chart rebuilds on upload.
  • Every reorg proposal now arrives with its sandbox scenario attached.
  • Quarterly talent review runs on the bench view.

Deliverable: Refresh routine and reorg review template.

Scale · Live from the HRMS

From a monthly Excel to a daily feed.

  • HRMS export replaces manual updates.
  • Role-based views: leaders see their own tree, HR sees all.
  • Cost and diversity lenses added with access controls.

Deliverable: Integration roadmap and access model.

Key Takeaways

What any HR team can borrow

01

One sheet beats thirty-four decks

When the org chart is generated from data HR already owns, it stays current without anyone redrawing a box.

02

Spans and layers are design choices

Once narrow spans and deep layers were visible, leaders fixed them in a quarter. They had simply never seen them.

03

Rehearse the reorg on screen

Seeing the layers, cost and people affected before the announcement turned reorg debates into choices between options.
