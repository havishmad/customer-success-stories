Knowing which sellers will leave, 90 days before they do

HMC built a Python machine-learning model on a leading life insurer's HR, sales and activity data that scores every front-line seller each month, explains why each one is at risk, and tells managers what to do about it.

The Challenge

By the time HR heard, the seller had gone

Front-line sales attrition was measured after the fact, explained by exit interviews and treated with one policy for everyone.

Early exits

Most leavers went in their first year, before recovering their hiring and licensing cost.

Lagging signals

Monthly attrition reports told leaders what had already happened.

Data in silos

HRMS, sales MIS, incentive files and CRM logins were never joined at seller level.

One fix for all

Retention spend went to everyone, or to whoever shouted loudest.

HMC Approach

From four data sources to a monthly risk list

Built in Python with HR, sales operations and channel heads; reviewed every month.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Join the seller's story

HRMS, sales MIS, incentive payouts and CRM activity at seller-month level.

  • Three years of joiners and leavers across channels.
  • Exit reasons coded from interviews.
  • Agreed the target: resigns in the next 90 days.

Deliverable: Analytical dataset and data dictionary.

Design · Features managers recognise

Every feature had to be something a manager could act on or explain.

  • Tenure, target achievement, incentive, persistency.
  • Manager changes, training, leaves, CRM logins.
  • Pay against band, commute, channel and zone.

Deliverable: Feature catalogue and leakage checks.

Build · Python models, explained

scikit-learn and LightGBM, explained with SHAP.

  • Logistic regression baseline, gradient boosting final.
  • Time-based validation; calibrated probabilities.
  • Reason codes and a retention action for each seller.

Deliverable: Scoring pipeline and risk desk.

Adopt · Monthly risk review

Agency and bancassurance first.

  • Risk list to branch and area managers on day 3.
  • One action per at-risk seller, tracked to closure.
  • Model refreshed monthly; drift monitored.

Deliverable: Manager playbook.

Scale · Every front-line seller

Direct, tele-sales and partnerships added.

  • Hiring scorecard from the same drivers.
  • Incentive design tested against predicted risk.
  • CHRO and channel head dashboard.

Deliverable: Roll-out plan.

Key Takeaways
What any sales-led business can borrow

    

      
01
Predict, then explain
A risk score without reasons gets ignored; reason codes turn it into a manager's to-do.

      
02
The first six months decide
Onboarding, early targets and a stable manager matter more than pay for new sellers.

      
03
Pick the threshold with money
Acting on everyone wastes budget; the cost curve shows where retention pays.
