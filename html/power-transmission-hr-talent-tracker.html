The Challenge

Every talent review started from scattered sheets

Talent reviews meant pulling each leader's ratings, risk and succession data from scattered HR sheets, then rebuilding the same slides by hand.

Data in many places

Ratings, 360 scores, compensation, LTIP and succession sat in different files owned by different teams.

History hard to see

Rating trends across FY24–FY26 and performance by half-year had to be stitched together for each leader.

Risk flagged too late

Risk of loss, impact of loss and mobility were discussed in the room, not visible on the page beforehand.

Slides by hand

Each review cycle meant rebuilding one-pagers in PowerPoint and re-checking every number.

HMC Approach

We taught the pattern; HR built the tool

HMC taught the HR team to prompt GenAI into building working tools: data fields, scoring rules, a one-page layout and bulk export.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · What does a talent review need on one page?

We started from the review conversation, not the data.

  • Listed the questions leadership asks about each executive in a talent review.
  • Mapped where each answer lived: HRMS, rating sheets, 360 reports, succession files.
  • Agreed what stays confidential and needs masking, such as compensation and LTIP.

Deliverable: Field inventory and source map.

Design · Fields, scores, flags

The HR team wrote the rules in plain language before any build.

  • Field dictionary: profile, talent profile, performance history, leadership impact, readiness, successors, actions.
  • Scoring rules: TR rating by year, half-yearly performance, 360 and manager-climate trends.
  • Flags: risk of loss, impact of loss, willingness to relocate.

Deliverable: Field dictionary and scoring rules.

Build · Prompting GenAI into a working tool

In hands-on workshops, the HR team prompted the scorecard into existence themselves.

  • Prompted the one-page layout: dark profile panel, talent profile, readiness, actions, successors.
  • Added bulk upload for many employees, single upload, and trend toggles.
  • Added export: bulk or selected PDF, selected PPT and Excel.

Deliverable: Executive Talent Scorecard, built by HR.

Adopt · Run the talent review on it

Used for the Talent Review 2025–2026 cycle.

  • Scorecards bulk-generated from the data extract and checked by HR business partners.
  • Review packs exported straight to PPT and PDF for leadership.
  • Actions taken and actions proposed tracked on each leader's page.

Deliverable: Review pack for the 2025–26 cycle.

Scale · The pattern, not just the tool

The same five-step pattern now drives other HR tools.

  • Fields → Score → Flag → Layout → Export, reused for new HR trackers.
  • Next-cycle refresh by re-uploading the extract.
  • HR team owns changes without waiting for IT.

Deliverable: Reusable tool-building playbook.

Fields on every scorecard

  • Profile: business unit, grade, manager, location, tenure in role and organisation, total experience, function
  • Reward: compensation by FY and unvested LTIP, with masking toggles; last promotion date
  • Talent profile: TR rating FY24–FY26, previous-cycle top talent, half-yearly performance history
  • Retention & mobility: risk of loss, impact of loss, willing to relocate
  • Leadership impact: 360 assessment, manager climate and OPTIM manager scores, with trends
  • Readiness: roles ready now and in 2–3 years; core strengths and development areas
  • Succession & actions: potential successors with readiness; actions taken and proposed

Key Takeaways

What transfers to any HR team

01

Teach the pattern, not the tool

Fields, Score, Flag, Layout, Export. Once HR knew the steps, they could prompt new tools without waiting for IT.

02

Design for the review conversation

Start from what leadership asks about each executive. The page layout follows those questions.

03

Build confidentiality in from day one

Masking toggles for compensation and LTIP made the same scorecard safe to use in wider forums.
