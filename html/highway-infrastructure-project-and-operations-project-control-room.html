The Challenge

Many sites, one view that came too late

Leadership saw progress, machines, bids and safety in separate reports, weeks after the fact.

Progress in WhatsApp

Daily reports arrived as texts, voice notes and photos; the monthly review was the first consolidated view.

Idle machines, missing diesel

Telematics existed but nobody compared hours, idle time and fuel issued across sites.

Three days per tender

Each RFP ran to hundreds of pages; risky clauses surfaced late in the bid cycle.

Safety after the incident

Observations were logged on paper; patterns across sites were invisible.

HMC Approach

Built with site, plant, tendering and HSE teams

One control room, four lenses, starting from data the teams already produced.

Design · Chainage as the common key

Every record tied to a project, a chainage and a date.

  • Activity sequence and weights per pavement layer.
  • Fuel norms by machine type; utilisation on available hours.
  • Clause risk rules agreed with the tendering head.

Deliverable: Data model and rule book.

Build · Four modules, one control room

GenAI where reading is the bottleneck, rules where the numbers decide.

  • Report, voice and photo reading into structured progress.
  • Fleet, fuel and redeployment analytics on a map.
  • Tender reader, estimate and bid score; photo safety checks.

Deliverable: Control room prototype.

Adopt · Three pilot projects

Site teams kept sending reports the way they always had.

  • Weekly CMD summary generated from the control room.
  • Plant head reviews fuel flags every morning.
  • Bid committee starts from the tender reader output.

Deliverable: Pilot and playbook.

Scale · Every project, every machine

Rolled out across road, rail and new verticals.

  • All projects on the same chainage model.
  • Telematics feeds for the full fleet.
  • Safety scorecard in the monthly board pack.

Deliverable: Roll-out plan.
