The Challenge

"Delivered on time" but the customer still wasn't happy

Customer feedback arrived as free text. The SLA guidelines lived in a separate document. Nobody could say which promises were being kept and which were hurting NPS.

Feedback as free text

Verbatims mixed delivery, packaging, app and pricing in one paragraph. Manual tagging was slow and inconsistent.

SLA met, NPS missed

Deliveries landed inside 2–5 days, yet customers compared the retailer with faster platforms and felt let down.

Issues across six teams

Logistics, app, catalogue, support, returns and packaging each owned part of the experience, and each saw only its own slice.

Breaches hidden in averages

Zero-tolerance SLAs, such as wrong items or tampered packaging, were breached without anyone tracking them against the guideline.

HMC Approach

Make every comment answer to an SLA

Two inputs, customer comments and SLA guidelines, turned into one decision-ready report.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Gather the two sources of truth

The customer's voice and the company's promise, side by side.

  • Collected customer feedback comments from post-delivery surveys.
  • Consolidated SLA guidelines into six dimensions: delivery, order communication, customer support, returns and refunds, product quality, and experience.
  • Agreed what counts as "met", "perception gap", "partial breach" and "breach".

Deliverable: Comment set and a single SLA guideline sheet.

Design · A mapping frame for GenAI

Each comment gets the same structured treatment.

  • For every comment: SLA dimension, status, customer observation and SLA implication.
  • Per SLA dimension: guideline, status, evidence and the comment numbers behind it.
  • Recommendations split into immediate fixes, short-term gaps and strategic moves.

Deliverable: Prompt frame and output template.

Build · From verbatims to a four-tab report

Claude read every comment against the guidelines and produced the report.

  • Executive summary with six headline counts and a key takeaway.
  • Comment-by-comment SLA mapping, with evidence linked to the guideline.
  • SLA scorecard across six dimensions, plus prioritised recommendations.

Deliverable: Feedback vs SLA HTML report: Summary, Comment Analysis, SLA Scorecard, Recommendations.

Adopt · One review, six owners

The report became the agenda for a cross-functional CX review.

  • Each breached SLA was assigned to an owning team with a fix date.
  • Perception gaps went to marketing and product for messaging and options.
  • "Comment 9 moments" were defined as the experience standard to replicate.

Deliverable: Action log with owners and dates.

Scale · Run it on every month's verbatims

The same frame now runs on the full NPS comment stream.

  • Monthly run on all survey verbatims, not a sample.
  • Perception-gap share tracked alongside NPS and SLA attainment.
  • SLA guidelines updated when customer expectations move.

Deliverable: Monthly Feedback vs SLA pack.

Key Takeaways

What transfers to any customer-facing business

01

An SLA met is not a customer won

13 of 15 deliveries were on time, yet 8 customers still felt short-changed. Competitors set the expectation, not the SLA document.

02

Fix upstream, not after delivery

Zero-tolerance SLAs need prevention: scan-verified dispatch and packaging checks at handoff, not better apologies.

03

Engineer the best moments

One fully satisfied customer showed the standard: fast, secure, as described. Design for more of those on purpose.
