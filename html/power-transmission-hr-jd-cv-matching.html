The Challenge

Specialist roles, hundreds of CVs, little time

Roles like EHV substation execution or transmission line construction need precise experience. Recruiters were reading every CV by hand, and hiring managers often met candidates without a clear view of fit, gaps or what to probe.

Volume of CVs

Each requisition drew large applicant pools from portals, referrals and agencies, in mixed formats.

Keyword screening misses

Simple keyword filters passed buzzword-heavy CVs and missed strong candidates who described work differently.

Red flags found late

Gaps, short tenures and unsupported claims surfaced in interviews, not before them.

Generic interviews

Panels used the same question list for everyone, so strong claims went untested.

HMC Approach

Explainable scoring first, GenAI on top

Rules the TA team can read and adjust, with GenAI for summaries, questions and conversation.

Design · A transparent score

Seven criteria, weighted by the hiring manager.

  • Skills taxonomy for power, projects and HR roles.
  • Evidence-based matching: a skill counts fully only when shown in work history.
  • Fair-use rules: no scoring on age, gender, religion or photos.

Deliverable: Scoring model and guardrails.

Build · The copilot

One screen for the recruiter.

  • CV upload (PDF, Word, text) and JD library.
  • Score, evidence highlights, career timeline.
  • Personalised questions, red flags and a CV chatbot, with a large language model for live answers.

Deliverable: Working copilot.

Adopt · Pilot on live requisitions

Human decision, machine preparation.

Key Takeaways

What any TA team can borrow

01

Evidence beats keywords

Count a skill fully only when the CV shows it being used; lists alone earn partial credit.

02

Let the hiring manager own the weights

A transparent score with adjustable weights earns trust that a black box never will.

03

Prepare the panel, don't replace it

Personalised questions and red flags make interviews sharper; people still make the call.
