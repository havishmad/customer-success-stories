The Challenge

Procedures written for auditors, not for the bay

An LPG bottling plant runs on strict procedures, from tanker receipt to cylinder dispatch. The procedures existed, but in a form that was hard to follow in the moment and slow to keep current.

Long PDFs

Each SOP ran to 20 to 40 pages of text. Operators knew the gist; few could find the exact step under pressure.

Branches buried in prose

"If gas is detected, then…" sat in paragraph six. Decision points and hand-offs between roles were hard to see.

Slow to change

After a near-miss or a management-of-change approval, updating, approving and re-printing an SOP took about three weeks.

Old copies in the field

Laminated copies at bays and in contractor packs drifted out of date, and audits kept flagging the gap.

HMC Approach

SOPs as code: one source, many views

Each SOP became a short text file in Mermaid. The plant's subject-matter experts own the content; the diagrams, playback and versions follow automatically.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · Walk the plant with the SOP in hand

We followed tankers, filling lines and dispatch with operators and safety officers.

  • Inventoried 48 operating and safety SOPs across receipt, storage, filling, dispatch and maintenance.
  • Marked where people hesitated: decision points, hand-offs and rarely used emergency branches.
  • Reviewed audit observations and near-miss reports linked to procedures.

Deliverable: SOP inventory with priority by risk and use.

Design · Pick the right picture for each question

Different questions need different diagrams, all from the same SOP.

  • Flowchart for "what do I do next?" and sequence for "who tells whom?".
  • State diagram for "where is this tanker now?" and Gantt for shutdown plans.
  • Mind map to navigate the SOP library and timeline for the change history.

Deliverable: Diagram standard, role colours and naming rules.

Build · GenAI drafts, experts approve

Claude converted each PDF into Mermaid code and step metadata.

  • Every step tagged with owner, time, PPE, checks and the source clause.
  • Scenario switches for the branches that matter: gas detected, earthing fault, storm warning.
  • Plant SMEs reviewed every diagram against the source SOP before release.

Deliverable: SOP studio with playback, scenarios and role filters.

Adopt · At the bay, in the control room, in training

The same SOP shows up where the work happens.

  • QR codes at bays open the live flowchart on a phone or tablet.
  • Control room plays the sequence view during unloading.
  • Inductions use step-by-step playback instead of slides.

Deliverable: Bay QR rollout and training playbook.

Scale · Change once, update everywhere

Management of change now ends with a code edit, not a reprint.

  • Approved changes merged into the SOP file; every view updates the same day.
  • Version timeline kept for audit, with the reason for each change.
  • Template extended to other plants and to terminal operations.

Deliverable: SOP-as-code governance and rollout plan.

Key Takeaways

What transfers to any operations team

01

Write the SOP once, as code

A short text file can become every diagram a team needs. Change the file and every view follows.

02

Show the branches

Most incidents happen off the happy path. Making "what if" visible, and playable, is where the value is.

03

GenAI drafts, experts own

Conversion is fast with AI. Trust comes from plant experts approving every step and every change.
