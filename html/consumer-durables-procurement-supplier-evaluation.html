The Challenge

Sourcing decisions on scattered data

Quality lived in the QMS, delivery in the ERP, prices in emails and spreadsheets. Supplier reviews were long, subjective and hard to compare across categories.

Data in silos

Defect rates, on-time delivery, prices and certificates sat in different systems and files.

Subjective selection

Rankings depended on who was in the room; criteria and weights changed from review to review.

Concentration risk

Critical parts depended on one supplier or one country, with little view of the exposure.

Quotes hard to compare

Different currencies, Incoterms and payment terms made true landed cost slow to work out.

HMC Approach

Agree the rules, then let the data rank

A transparent method the category heads sign off, applied the same way to every supplier.

Select a step to see what happened.

Discover · How suppliers were judged

Interviews with category managers, quality and stores.

  • Data sources for quality, delivery, price, capacity and certificates mapped.
  • Past sourcing decisions and their outcomes reviewed.
  • Critical components and single-source exposure listed.

Deliverable: Criteria catalogue and data map.

Design · A matrix everyone signs

Eight criteria, clear formulas.

  • Scoring rules from raw metrics (defects per million, on-time-in-full, price index).
  • Weights agreed with pairwise comparison (AHP) and a consistency check.
  • Segmentation rules: partner, develop, maintain, exit.

Deliverable: Signed-off evaluation framework.

Build · The sourcing workspace

From scorecard to sourcing decision.

  • Automated monthly scorecards from ERP and QMS extracts.
  • Volume split optimiser and disruption scenarios.
  • GenAI quote reader and supplier review letters.

Deliverable: Working tool and Power BI views.

Adopt · Quarterly reviews on the tool

Same data in every meeting.

Key Takeaways

What any procurement team can borrow

01

Agree the weights before the scores

When the committee signs the weights first, the ranking is accepted, not argued.

02

Compare on landed cost

The cheapest quote on paper is often not the cheapest at the plant gate.

03

Design for a second source

Know the cover days and the switch before the disruption, not after.
