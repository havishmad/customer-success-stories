The Challenge

Copper hit records. The buying calendar didn't care.

Copper is one of Amber's largest raw-material costs, in heat-exchanger tubes, motor windings and interconnecting pipes. In 2025–26 it set record after record, driven by mine accidents, US tariff moves and the dollar. Buying decisions were still made from a weekly email and gut feel.

Record volatility

LME copper went from under $11,000 to nearly $14,900 a tonne in twelve months, with daily swings of 3–4%.

Drivers everywhere

A mudslide in Indonesia, a strike in Chile, a tariff memo in Washington, a PMI in Beijing. Nobody had the full picture.

Reactive buying

Tubes and cathode were bought to the production calendar, so Amber often paid above the monthly LME average in peak AC season.

Cost impact unclear

Sales and finance asked what a $500 move meant per AC and per quarter. The answer took days.

A transparent factor model: start from today's price, add each driver's effect over six months, and show the range. Change a lever and the forecast, the 3D timeline and the buying advice all update.
